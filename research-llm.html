Research / Language-model evaluation

Education technology · LLM evaluation

LLM performance on standardized examinations.

How reliable are language models when a test demands careful reading, multi-step reasoning, and accurate execution? We compared four models across benchmarks and exam-style questions to find out.

At a glance

Measuring reliability beyond a single score.

4models compared
~1,500benchmark questions
4custom exam datasets
4prompt styles tested

The study evaluates ChatGPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, ChatGLM-4.5, and DeepSeek across mathematics, physics, computer science, and geography. It pairs questions from MMLU, LogiQA, and C-Eval with exam datasets drawn from AP, A-Level, Gaokao, and TMUA papers.

Rather than asking only which model scores highest, we examined how prompt design changes accuracy, how models behave across complete papers, and what kinds of mistakes persist.

Study design

From benchmark questions to full exam conditions.

What we built and tested

  • Four exam datasets using mainly multiple-choice questions from AP, A-Level, Gaokao, and TMUA papers.
  • A LaTeX-to-JSON workflow that preserves question text, options, formulas, image references, and traceable question IDs, followed by consistency checks.
  • Four prompting styles: direct answer, role-playing, step-by-step reasoning, and worked examples.
  • Full-paper simulations for GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro on Gaokao, AP, and A-Level exams.

Why it matters

Public benchmarks often present isolated questions. Exam papers add structure, a progression of difficulty, and a need for sustained accuracy. Keeping the source questions and answer keys traceable makes the comparison more meaningful for educational assessment.

Multiple-choice scoring offers a clear answer key, but it also narrows what the evaluation can say about open-ended student work.

Reported results

Strong scores still hide distinct failure modes.

Overall performance

Gemini 2.5 Pro

The paper reports Gemini as the strongest overall performer across the tested tasks. Results still vary substantially by subject and exam.

Prompt effect

Logic +28.5%

Using an optimized prompt rather than a direct-answer prompt produced the paper's largest reported accuracy improvement, on logic questions.

Error analysis

Look at the kind of mistake, not just the score.

Six error categories

  • Reading and parsing: missed words, entities, or language cues.
  • Computation and formulas: arithmetic, algebra, or statistics mistakes.
  • Reasoning: broken inference chains or overgeneralization.
  • Strategy and instruction-following: ignored format or method requirements.
  • Factuality and hallucination: fabricated or misremembered facts.
  • Dataset and label issues: ambiguous questions or answer keys.

Different model patterns

On the full exams, GPT-5 made more reasoning-chain errors, while Gemini's errors more often involved computation or formulas. In the broader error analysis, DeepSeek's failures clustered around initial reading and parsing; ChatGLM showed more difficulty following strict instructions.

Reading and parsing remained a shared bottleneck across models. A polished explanation cannot compensate for misunderstanding the question.

Research direction

The case for a hybrid model.

The study proposes combining complementary strengths: route stages of a problem to models suited to reasoning, computation, or procedural checking, then validate the answer. This is a future architecture suggested by the error profiles, not a system built or evaluated in this paper.

The next steps include testing more open-ended questions, multimodal problems, and new model versions. The present results are limited by a focus on multiple-choice, mostly text-based questions and by the judgment involved in manually classifying errors.

Research takeaway

For learning tools, reliability means knowing when to verify an answer as well as when to provide one.
